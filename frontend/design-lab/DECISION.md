# Design Lab Decision (Phase 9)

Three materially different real candidates were built and rendered at
1440x900 (per Part 19/20 of the Phase 9 spec):

- **A — Bulletin Terminal** (`a-bulletin-terminal.html`): Meridian-inspired
  masthead/bulletin metadata bar, bracket-style active nav (`[COMMAND]`),
  editorial mixed-weight headline (bold white + bold gray same line), an
  inverted-polarity (dark vs. bright) before/after comparison panel for the
  chosen-vs-alternative path.
- **B — Neo-Brutalist Broadcast** (`b-neobrutalist-broadcast.html`): the
  prior Phase 8.3 direction (Bento Dashboard-inspired) - massive condensed
  headline, ghost watermark, literal flat bars as the chart object.
- **C — Quant Workstation** (`c-quant-workstation.html`): Financial-Markets-
  Table/System-Monitor-inspired - monospace-first, a dense real 3-path
  comparison table as the dominant object, smaller headline.

## Decision: A — Bulletin Terminal

Real, direct comparison: A reads as the most genuinely distinctive and
premium of the three - the inverted panel and masthead bar are real, fresh
devices neither B nor C have, and B in particular now reads as comparatively
generic once seen next to A (confirms the user's own repeated "still looks
like a SaaS dashboard" reaction to that direction). C is real and useful as
a DENSITY reference (the 3-path comparison table) but its own overall
composition is the least visually distinctive of the three.

**A becomes the real foundation for COMMAND**, and its devices (masthead
bar, mixed-weight headline, inverted panel, bracket nav) carry forward as
the shared grammar for the other six screens. C's dense comparison-table
idea is kept as a real, secondary device (used inside A's own structure
where a screen genuinely needs a multi-row comparison, e.g. PLAN's path
selector), not as a competing top-level direction.
